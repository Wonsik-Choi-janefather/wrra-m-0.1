WRRA TRANSFORMATION
Identical coherent carrier shifts on the two optical paths preserve visibility.
The declared candidate is a finite, SOURCE-label-controlled relative phase gate:
U_j=diag(exp[i g (2 pi/lambda) ell f_D(j)],1), where f_D is the centered
relative mean-dark-stress profile. Trial g=1 is not a derived optical coupling.
The path gate preserves photon-energy accounting, positivity and normalization;
it is not a derivation of a full Maxwell/SOURCE interaction Hamiltonian.
The control is initially I/128 with entropy ln128. Equal gates on both arms cancel.
L is differential coupling exposure, not automatically physical interferometer size.
Three new temporal policies are tested rather than silently selected:
  static: one phase label throughout a run, 128-state ensemble across runs;
  independent: one fresh independent phase register per physical shutter;
  cyclic: one-step label advancement on a 128-state cycle.
Independent preparation counts N fresh registers and N ln128 initial entropy.
Cyclic advancement is a candidate correlation policy; its SOURCE preparation
drive/energy costs are not derived. A phase-label lag is not measured time.

ASSESSMENT
The inherited coherent shift adds no dephasing. A new conditional finite-state
phase extension is executed and produces distinct, reproducible optical curves.
The snapshot does not select g or the temporal memory law. No unique empirical
optical coherence threshold is established. All 17 numerical controls pass;
they do not constitute observational validation of the new coupling.
The proposed target is wavelength-dependent visibility loss/revival and
cross-path phase correlation. It is not a derivation of the Heisenberg bound,
Lorentz completion, full Maxwell dynamics or a universal macroscopic collapse.
