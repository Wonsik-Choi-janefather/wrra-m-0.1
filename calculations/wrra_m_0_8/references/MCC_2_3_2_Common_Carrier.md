# Chapter 11 The Common Carrier and Fifteen Channels

The next problem was internal degrees of freedom. Describing the chiral matter of one generation requires multiple directions with different charges and representations. Rather than dispersing fifteen channels into separate worlds, the research explored a structure in which one common continuum carries them all.

The common carrier D\_C is a device for placing all fifteen directions under the same transport law. Channels may carry different internal labels, but local transport, kinematics, and boundary recording share a common skeleton. This applies minimal computation's principle of reuse to particle content.

_D\_C : ⊕ᵢ₌₁¹⁵ H\_i → ⊕ᵢ₌₁¹⁵ H\_i_ (2.12)

The early democratic rank-one coupling carried only one bright combination and left the other fourteen directions dark. An aesthetically symmetric form does not automatically create sufficient physical rank. This failure was recorded as a counterexample that had to be closed.

$$
rank(D_C│band) = 15
$$

_Equation (2.13)_

The conservative response was not to abandon the common carrier but to add couplings that open rank. One continuum must possess full band rank while preserving channel-specific charges and selection rules. Commonality and distinguishability must be satisfied simultaneously.

The serial-rotor idea also arose here. It is not a sequential switch that mechanically turns on channels one at a time, but a structure in which an autonomously rotating rank-one port relates to the full set of channels through phase encoding. It is phase-coded seriality over common transport, not a temporally ordered list.

$$
P(φ) = \|v(φ)⟩⟨v(φ)\|, rank P(φ)=1
$$

_Equation (2.14)_

The fifteen channels do not mean that all final particle masses and mixings have been explained. They are a candidate boundary record for the chiral carrier of one generation; generation replication, Yukawa couplings, quark mixing, strong-coupling dynamics, and the upstream generation rule for mass remain separate layers. Chapter 30 nevertheless identifies and freezes low-integer quantum relations in observed first-generation values and proposes candidates for the absolute neutrino masses, which have not yet been measured directly. This is a phenomenological prediction awaiting test, not an independent upstream derivation.
